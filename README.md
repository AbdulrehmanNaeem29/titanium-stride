# Performance Pedigree

You are acting as three specialists combined into one:

A world-class sports/performance-coaching brand web designer who has designed award-winning websites (Awwwards / FWA-tier quality) for elite athletic coaching and training brands — think the energy and precision of a professional sports-performance website, applied to equestrian training.

A senior UX strategist and CRO (conversion rate optimization) expert who understands that this business sells a service and a relationship (training, lessons, coaching), not a product — so the entire site must be built around building trust in the trainer's expertise and making it effortless to book a lesson or enquire about training.

A full-stack front-end developer fluent in modern responsive web development (React/Next.js or clean semantic HTML/CSS/JS — pick whichever stack Replit's environment already supports best), who writes pixel-perfect, fully responsive, fast-loading code.

Before writing any code, think like a creative director: study the brief below, mentally benchmark it against the best professional equestrian training/coaching websites and elite personal-training/sports-performance coach websites in the world, and design something that makes a prospective student or horse owner feel: "this trainer is serious, credentialed, and will genuinely improve my/my horse's performance."

This is not a generic template job. Do not use generic Bootstrap-card layouts, default stock-photo hero sliders, or cookie-cutter local-business templates. Every section should feel dynamic, athletic, and professional — closer to a premium sports-coaching or performance-brand website than a typical small-business site.

1. THE BUSINESS (read carefully — this is who we are designing for)

Business name: Titanium Equestrian LLC Tagline: "Partnership · Progress · Performance" What they do: Horse training and riding lessons, with an eventing-based foundation (meaning the training philosophy is rooted in classical eventing principles — flatwork/dressage fundamentals, gymnastic jumping, and cross-country education — even for students or horses who may ultimately compete in other disciplines or simply want a well-rounded, correctly trained partnership). Category: Horse trainer / riding instructor (service-based business, not a horse-sales business) Location context: Based in Washington State, USA (Pacific Northwest — area code 253, Tacoma/Pierce County area) Price positioning: ££ — professional, mid-to-upper market coaching pricing, not budget lessons Credibility signal: Sponsored by Antarès Sellier USA — Antarès is a globally respected premium French saddle maker used by elite competitors; being a sponsored trainer is a genuine mark of quality and credibility in the equestrian world and should be featured as a trust badge, not buried. Brand identity (already established — the website MUST extend this, not replace it): A modern, athletic logo featuring a stylized teal-and-navy horse head/mane inside a circular motif, paired with a clean, confident serif wordmark ("TITANIUM EQUESTRIAN") and the tagline "Partnership · Progress · Performance" in teal. This is a polished, performance-driven, contemporary brand — not a rustic/countryside farm brand and not an ultra-luxury boutique. It should feel more like a premium athletic/coaching brand (think the crispness of a modern sports academy) applied to equestrian sport.

Positioning to communicate throughout the site: Titanium Equestrian is a professional coaching and training service built on a proven eventing foundation, helping riders and horses build genuine partnership and measurable progress toward real performance goals — whether that's a nervous first-time rider gaining confidence, an ambitious junior aiming for recognized competition, or a horse owner who wants their horse professionally trained/started/reschooled. The brand voice should be confident, disciplined, encouraging, and expert — like a respected coach, not a casual hobby barn.

Pages to design in this phase (only these — do not build anything beyond this list):

3. VISUAL DESIGN DIRECTION

3.1 Competitor & Category Benchmarking (do this mentally before designing)

Before designing, internally benchmark against the visual standard of:

Top professional eventing/dressage/show-jumping coaching businesses and training barns with strong web presences (clean, athletic, credibility-driven)

Elite personal-training and sports-performance coach websites (the way they present programs, credentials, and client transformations/results)

Sponsored-athlete personal brand sites, since this trainer is a sponsored rider — that same "professional athlete" polish should come through

Avoid: cluttered classifieds-style layouts, generic "local horse barn" templates, stock clip-art, dated skeuomorphic design, or anything that undersells the sponsorship credibility and coaching expertise on offer.

3.2 Color Palette

Use the existing brand palette from the logo as the foundation — do not invent a new palette:

Primary: The brand teal (a bright, confident teal/turquoise, approximately in the #1E7F84–#2A9DA0 range) — used for accents, buttons, links, icons, and section highlights

Secondary/Dark: A deep navy/near-black (matching the dark horse-head linework and wordmark) — used for headers, footers, and primary text/backgrounds for strong contrast

Neutral base: Clean white and light warm-grey for main content backgrounds — this brand should feel crisp and modern, not rustic

Text: Deep navy/charcoal for body copy

This teal + navy + white combination should be used consistently across every page, mirroring the existing logo exactly, so the site feels like a natural digital extension of the brand that's already been designed

3.3 Typography

Headings: A confident, clean serif or modern slab-serif (echoing the elegant capitalized serif used in the "TITANIUM EQUESTRIAN" wordmark) for a premium, established feel

Body text: A crisp, modern sans-serif for excellent readability and a contemporary, athletic edge

Use wide letter-spacing on uppercase labels/eyebrow text (matching "PARTNERSHIP · PROGRESS · PERFORMANCE" styling) as a consistent design motif site-wide — this three-word rhythm can even be echoed structurally (e.g. 3-pillar sections)

3.4 Layout & Feel

Clean, high-contrast, athletic layout — generous white space but with a more energetic, dynamic feel than a rustic farm site (motion, diagonal section dividers, dynamic image cropping showing horses/riders mid-movement — jumping, galloping, cross-country)

Action-oriented photography as the hero of nearly every section: horse and rider jumping, cantering cross-country, in dressage work — capturing "progress and performance" visually

The Antarès Sellier sponsorship should appear as a clean, professional trust badge/logo mention (e.g. in the hero area or a dedicated "Proudly Sponsored By" strip) — treat it the way a sponsored athlete site would treat a major sponsor logo

Subtle scroll-triggered fade/slide-in animations; consider a subtle parallax or motion effect on the hero to reinforce "performance" energy

Circular framing motifs echoing the circular horse-head logo mark can be used as a recurring design element (e.g. circular photo crops, circular icon backgrounds)

3.5 Responsiveness

Fully responsive and pixel-tested for: large desktop, laptop, tablet (portrait & landscape), and mobile

Elegant slide-in/hamburger mobile menu matching the brand style

Touch-friendly tap targets, readable font sizes on mobile

Sticky/condensed header on scroll with a persistent "Book a Lesson" or "Enquire" CTA button, especially on mobile

6. HOME / LANDING PAGE — SECTION BY SECTION

Design each of these as a distinct, dynamic, well-composed section:

Hero Section: Full-viewport, high-impact action photo or subtle video/motion background of horse and rider mid-jump or in cross-country motion. Overlay the tagline "Partnership · Progress · Performance," a short compelling sub-line about eventing-based training for horse and rider, and a primary CTA ("Book a Lesson") plus a secondary CTA ("View Training Programs").

Sponsorship & Credibility Strip: Immediately below the hero — a clean strip featuring "Proudly Sponsored by Antarès Sellier USA" plus 2–3 other quick credibility markers (e.g. years of experience, eventing levels competed/trained, number of students coached) presented as confident stat badges.

The Titanium Method / Philosophy: A section explaining the eventing-based training foundation — why this approach builds better, more well-rounded horses and riders regardless of their ultimate discipline. Structure this as three pillars matching the tagline — "Partnership" (horse-and-rider relationship/trust), "Progress" (structured, measurable skill-building), "Performance" (competition/goal-readiness) — each with a short description and icon.

Training Programs Preview: A visually strong preview grid of the core offerings (e.g. Private Lessons, Group Lessons, Horse Training/Starting, Competition Coaching, Clinics) with a "View All Programs" CTA linking to the full Training Programs page.

Meet the Trainer (teaser): A short, confident introduction to the trainer with a strong action photo, 2–3 sentences of bio/credentials, and a "Learn More About Me" CTA to the full About page.

Results & Testimonials (teaser): A rotating carousel of short student/client testimonials and any competition results or student achievements, with a "Read More Success Stories" CTA to the full Testimonials page.

Who This Is For: A short section addressing the range of students served (e.g. "New to eventing," "Building confidence," "Competing at recognized levels," "Horses in need of professional training/restarting") so visitors immediately self-identify with a program.

Final CTA Band: A full-width navy-and-teal closing banner just above the footer ("Ready to build your partnership, progress, and performance?") with a "Book a Lesson" button — every page should end with this same conversion band.

7. TRAINING PROGRAMS & LESSONS PAGE (the most important page — design this with the most care; this is the equivalent of a "product page" for a service business)

This page must give a prospective student or horse owner everything they need to understand the offerings and choose one to book. Structure it like a premium coaching-program page, not a plain bullet list. Include, for each program/service offered:

Program name and clear category (e.g. Private Riding Lessons, Group Lessons, Horse Training & Starting, Eventing/Competition Coaching, Clinics & Camps)

Who it's for (skill level, age group, goals — e.g. "beginner to intermediate riders," "horses being started or reschooled," "competitive riders preparing for recognized events")

What's included (lesson length/frequency, on-site vs. off-site, use of school horses if applicable, video review, goal-setting/progress tracking)

The eventing-based approach applied to that program (a short note on how the flatwork/jumping/cross-country foundation shows up in that specific offering)

Pricing indication (exact price if provided, or "Contact for pricing/packages" — keep consistent with the ££ positioning: professional, not bargain-priced)

A clear "Book This Program" / "Enquire" CTA per program

Additional elements on this page:

A video section showing training in action (lessons, horse-starting sessions, or competition clips) — critical for a coaching business, since prospective clients want to see teaching/riding style before committing

A short FAQ block addressing common questions (e.g. "Do I need my own horse?", "What levels do you teach?", "Do you travel to clients or is this at a specific facility?")

A closing CTA band matching the site-wide style

8. ABOUT THE TRAINER / PHILOSOPHY PAGE

Full trainer bio: background, riding/competition history, certifications, years of experience, and the story of how Titanium Equestrian LLC was founded

A deeper explanation of the eventing-based training philosophy and why it produces better-rounded horses and more capable, confident riders

The Antarès Sellier sponsorship explained in a bit more depth (what it represents, how it reflects the quality of horsemanship here)

Strong action photography of the trainer riding/competing/teaching

End with the same CTA band as the homepage

9. TESTIMONIALS & RESULTS PAGE

A well-designed grid or carousel of client/student testimonials (name, photo if available, what they achieved, star rating if used)

A results/achievements section — competition placings, student progress milestones, before/after horse-training outcomes

Designed to build strong social proof and trust before a prospective client commits to booking

10. CONTACT / BOOK A LESSON PAGE

A refined enquiry/booking form (Name, Email, Phone, Program of Interest [dropdown], Experience Level, Message)

Direct contact details clearly displayed: phone (+1 253-263-2021)

Service area/location note (Washington State / Pacific Northwest — adjust once exact facility address is provided)

A note on scheduling (e.g. response time, how booking works)

Social/Instagram/Facebook click-through icons

11. ADDITIONAL PROFESSIONAL REQUIREMENTS (add anything else in this category that a top-1% agency would include, even if not explicitly listed above)

SEO fundamentals: semantic HTML structure, proper heading hierarchy, meta title/description placeholders per page, descriptive alt text on all images, clean URL structure (e.g. /training-programs, /about)

Fast loading: optimized/compressed images, lazy-loading below-the-fold images and video media

Accessibility: sufficient color contrast between teal/navy/white, keyboard-navigable menus, alt text, readable font sizes

Micro-interactions: smooth hover states on buttons/cards/links, subtle scroll animations, smooth page transitions

Consistent iconography style (clean line icons in teal, matching the logo's linework style)

A cohesive favicon and browser tab title using the brand mark

Cross-browser and cross-device consistency (desktop, tablet, mobile)

Design the whole experience so that if this exact site were shown to the trainer for the first time, their immediate reaction would be "this finally looks as professional and sponsor-worthy as my riding actually is" — that first impression is the single most important success metric for this project
